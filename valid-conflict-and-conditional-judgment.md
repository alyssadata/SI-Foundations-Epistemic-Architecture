# Valid Conflict and Conditional Judgment — Working Structure

**Origin:** Alyssa Solen  
**First introduced in current working record:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** original conceptual model / working hypothesis  
**Evidence status:** methodological proposal; not yet empirically validated as a complete theory of conflicting evidence  
**AI contribution:** formalization and structural decomposition from Alyssa Solen's originating rule that when two pieces of evidence are both valid yet conflict, there may be two answers rather than one evidence item being discarded.

## Core rule

Valid conflict does not imply that one side is invalid.

When two pieces of evidence are both:

- grounded,
- structurally relevant,
- validly measured,
- and genuinely in conflict,

then the correct judgment may preserve more than one answer.

Therefore:

```text
conflict != invalidity
multiple valid conditions may imply multiple valid answers
single-output pressure != epistemic justification
```

The evaluator should not force a single answer merely because the interface, benchmark, institution, or decision format expects one.

## Conditional truth

Two apparently conflicting answers may both be valid when they hold under different conditions, scopes, populations, times, positions, definitions, or causal pathways.

A stronger evaluation therefore asks:

```text
Under what conditions is A supported?
Under what conditions is B supported?
What variable partitions the two?
```

The result may take the form:

```text
A under condition X
B under condition Y
```

rather than:

```text
A OR B, choose one
```

## Judgment outputs

Judgment should be allowed to return at least three classes of output.

### 1. Single answer

The evaluated evidence sufficiently supports one option and the competing alternatives are not equally valid under the same conditions.

### 2. Multiple conditional answers

Two or more answers are supported, but under different identified conditions.

```text
A if X
B if Y
```

The task becomes identifying and preserving the condition boundary rather than collapsing the answers.

### 3. Unresolved branch

The evidence supports more than one live possibility, but the discriminating variable has not yet been measured or identified.

```text
A or B remains live
→ identify missing discriminating measurement
→ gather additional ground evidence
```

Suspending forced resolution is therefore a valid judgment state.

## Missing-variable interpretation

Valid conflict can reveal that the current model is missing a variable.

If two measurements are both legitimate but produce different results, the evaluator should search for the structural condition that partitions them.

Possible partitioning variables include:

- time
- location or position
- population
- environmental condition
- measurement method
- scope
- definition
- causal path
- initial state
- system configuration
- observer-access differences

The conflict can therefore increase understanding by exposing hidden structure.

## Anti-collapse rule

A major epistemic failure occurs when valid conflict is compressed into one answer without sufficient justification.

```text
multiple supported conditions
→ forced single answer
→ structural information loss
```

This is a form of collapse because the evaluator erases the distinction that explains why both evidence streams exist.

Therefore:

> Do not collapse valid conflict into a false single answer. Partition the conditions under which each answer holds.

## Relation to structural relevance

`structural-relevance.md` determines whether evidence bears on the proposition.

This file addresses what happens after two or more pieces of structurally relevant evidence survive that gate but do not converge.

The evaluator should then ask:

```text
Are the evidence items actually contradictory under the same conditions?
OR
Are they revealing different regions of the structure?
```

A disagreement only becomes a true contradiction if the evidence refers to the same proposition under the same relevant conditions.

Thus:

```text
different answer under different condition != contradiction
```

## Relation to judgment

Judgment is not always the act of reducing many possibilities to one.

A structurally adequate judgment may instead preserve the shape of the evidence.

Therefore:

```text
judgment may select one answer
judgment may preserve multiple conditional answers
judgment may suspend resolution pending a discriminating measurement
```

The quality of judgment depends on preserving the structure warranted by the evidence, not on producing artificial decisiveness.

## Single-output pressure

Systems are often rewarded for returning one answer even where the evidence remains conditional or unresolved.

This creates a failure mode:

```text
format pressure
→ premature collapse
→ apparent decisiveness
→ loss of valid structure
```

A well-formed epistemic system should resist this pressure when one answer is not justified.

Provisional invariant:

> Single-output pressure must not erase multi-condition truth.

## Diagnostic questions

When valid evidence conflicts, ask:

1. Do both evidence items measure the same proposition?
2. Were they obtained under the same relevant conditions?
3. Is there a hidden variable that partitions the outcomes?
4. Can the answers be expressed conditionally rather than competitively?
5. Is one piece actually weaker, or are both valid in different regions of the structure?
6. What new measurement would discriminate between the remaining possibilities?
7. Would forcing one answer destroy information that the evidence currently preserves?

## Provisional invariants

```text
conflict != invalidity
different conditions may support different answers
different answer under different condition != contradiction
multiple valid answers != evaluative failure
suspended judgment != failed judgment
single-output pressure != epistemic justification
```

## Working rule

When valid, relevant, grounded evidence conflicts:

```text
preserve both
→ inspect conditions
→ identify the partitioning variable if possible
→ return conditional answers when warranted
→ otherwise preserve the unresolved branch
→ seek the measurement that can discriminate further
```

The objective is not to force convergence.

The objective is to preserve the structure the evidence actually supports.
